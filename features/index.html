Where it goes

Every place your customers can get each issue

Your newsletter's name and issue number are on every one, wherever it lands.

Today

  • In their inbox: send the letter through the email tool you already use, or simply forward it.
  • On the web: each issue you publish gets a page of its own that anyone can open from a link, with no sign-in.
  • On a public page of all your issues, with the audio, the videos, the slides and the picture that passed their check. Geoff sets it up with you.
  • In a Slack or Discord channel: a short post with a link to each issue you publish, if you add a channel.
  • In a feed reader: a private RSS link to your published issues.
  • In your own inbox first: every issue lands with you, with every format on its page in your account.

Coming

  • coming Straight to each customer from your own address, for example news@yourbusiness.com, in the format each one picked, every issue.
  • coming In podcast apps: the audio of every issue as a podcast your customers can follow.
  • coming On Instagram and LinkedIn: posts made from each issue, ready for you to put up.
  • coming On your own website: a block that shows your latest issue.
  • coming On Telegram: each issue in a Telegram channel.

Change any line right on your issue. What you see is what they get.

Open any issue and press Edit in place. The issue opens exactly as your readers will see it, with every line you can change outlined. Click, type, Save. Discard puts back everything you have not saved.

  • What you can change: the headline, the opening paragraph, the inbox preview line, each section heading, the line on what each story means for your readers, each story summary, the quick links and the points to watch next.
  • What stays as published: story titles, links and the names of the websites they came from, so every fact still points home.
  • Plain words in, the full issue out: type as you would in an email. On Save, LongLens rewrites the whole issue with your words in it, in every copy it keeps.
  • Every version kept: the issue as LongLens first wrote it, and every save after that, so any change can be traced and copied back.
  • Locked once it goes out: once an issue is approved or sent, it stays exactly as it went.

Arrange your issue the way you would lay out a page

The Sections page puts every part of a daily newsletter in a list you can rearrange, with a live preview of your latest issue beside it.

  • Your order: drag a section, or use the arrows, to move it.
  • Only what you need: hide a section with one tick, or show at most a set number of stories in it.
  • Your headings: rename any section in your own words.
  • A note from you: add your own note, with its own heading, or a divider, anywhere in the issue.
  • Back to the usual: one button puts the usual sections back.

Your next issue uses the new layout in the email. Issues already written stay as they were.

Pictures that are yours to use

Turn on story pictures and your top stories each get a small picture beside them: a card drawn for your newsletter, in your colours, with the topic and the website the story came from. From one to six an issue, top stories only.

  • Never copied: LongLens never takes the picture from the article itself, so there is nothing to credit and nothing to take down.
  • Still reads well without them: the words come first, so the issue works in an inbox that hides pictures.
  • coming A picture generated for each story, in your own style, never the article's own photo.

Hit reply. The next issue listens.

Teach LongLens the way you would teach a new hire: reply to any issue in your own words. LongLens answers in the same thread with what it understood, and the next issue follows it.

  • Length: shorter, longer, or how many stories.
  • Topics: more of one thing, less of another.
  • Your customers: a new sentence about who they are and what matters to them.
  • Websites: paste a link to add one, name one to drop it.
  • Tone and layout: how it sounds, how each story is laid out.
  • Extras: add or drop the full audio or the slides.

Reply UNDO to take back the last change. LongLens acts only on replies from you: a forward to a colleague changes nothing.

Built from the websites, forums and videos you already trust

Every issue covers what is new on the websites, forums, YouTube channels and topics your industry follows. Name them to Geoff on your call, or paste a link into a reply any time.

  • Forward the newsletters you never open, or save a page from your phone or browser. What matters lands in your next issue under "You sent".
  • One tap to turn a website off, one more to turn it back on. LongLens never adds a website you turned off back by itself.
  • New websites, found for you: LongLens lists the ones your stories came from under "New sources for you", each with a way to remove it.

LongLens checks every story before your customers read it

  • What it means for them: most stories carry one line on why they matter to your customers, so the issue is never a link dump.
  • No repeats: a story they have already seen stays out until something changes, then comes back marked as an update.
  • Names and figures are checked against the original story.
  • Every link is tried before the issue goes out. A story whose only link is dead is left out, and the last line says how many links were checked and how many were dead.

An honest comparison

Three other ways to be the expert, and what each one costs you

By hand

Read the industry press every morning and write it up yourself. Full control. It costs the hours you do not have, and it dies the first busy week.

A newsletter platform

Great at sending and at growing a list. No idea what to say. You still find the news and write every word.

A personal AI summary

Fast, often free, and fine for keeping yourself informed. Written for you alone. Nobody else reads it, nobody learns what you know, and your name is nowhere on it.

LongLens tells your customers what today's news means for them, ready to send as text and often as audio. You keep the name, the last word and the customers.

What LongLens does not do

  • Write your own company news. LongLens works from what the industry press, the forums and the videos say about your field. Your own announcements are still yours to write.
  • Run ads or paid subscriptions. Use a newsletter platform built for that.
  • Replace you. Your judgment is the point. LongLens makes it reach further, issue after issue.
