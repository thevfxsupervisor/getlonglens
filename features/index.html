Change any line right on your issue. What you see is what they get.

Open any issue and press Edit in place. The issue opens exactly as your readers will see it, with every line you can change outlined. Click, type, Save. Discard puts back everything you have not saved.

  • What you can change: the headline, the opening paragraph, the inbox preview line, each section heading, the line on what each story means for your readers, each story summary, the quick links and the points to watch next.
  • What stays as published: story titles, links and the names of the websites they came from, so every fact still points home.
  • Type as you would in an email: on Save, the issue updates everywhere with your words.
  • Every version kept: the issue as LongLens first wrote it, and every save after that.
  • Locked once it goes out: once an issue is approved or sent, it stays exactly as it went.

Edit in place works on daily newsletters. A weekly newsletter has its own editor, section by section.

Lay out your issue the way you would lay out a page

On a daily newsletter, the Sections page lists every part of the issue, with a live preview of your latest issue beside it.

  • Your order: drag a section, or use the arrows, to move it.
  • Only what you need: hide a section with one tick, and show only as many topics as you want.
  • Your headings: rename the extra sections, such as quick links and what to watch, in your own words.
  • A note from you: add your own note, with its own heading, or a divider, anywhere in the issue.
  • Back to the usual: one button puts the usual sections back.

Your next issue uses the new layout. Issues already written stay as they were.

Pictures that are yours to use

Turn on story pictures and your top stories each get a small picture beside them: a card drawn for your newsletter, in your colours, with the topic and the website the story came from. From one to six an issue, top stories only.

  • Never copied: LongLens never takes the picture from the article itself, so there is nothing to credit and nothing to take down.

Reply in plain words to change the next issue

Teach LongLens the way you would brief a new hire: reply to any issue in your own words. LongLens answers in the same thread with what it understood, and the next issue follows it.

  • Length: shorter, longer, or how many stories.
  • Topics: more of one thing, less of another.
  • Your readers: a new sentence about who they are and what matters to them.
  • Websites: paste a link to add one, name one to drop it.
  • Tone and layout: how it sounds, how each story is laid out.
  • Formats: add or drop the two-voice audio.

Reply UNDO to take back the last change. LongLens acts only on replies from you: a forward to a colleague changes nothing.

Built from the websites, forums and videos your field already trusts

Every issue covers what is new on the websites, forums, YouTube channels and topics your field follows. Name them when you start, or paste a link into a reply any time.

  • Forward the newsletters you never open, or save a page from your phone or browser. What matters lands in your next issue under "You sent".
  • One tap to turn a website off, one more to turn it back on. LongLens never adds a website you turned off back by itself.
  • New websites, found for you: LongLens lists the ones your stories came from, each with a way to remove it.

LongLens checks every story before your readers see it

  • What it means for them: most stories carry one line on why they matter to your readers, so the issue is never a list of links.
  • No repeats: a story they have already seen stays out until something changes, then comes back marked as an update.
  • Names and figures are checked against the original story.
  • Links are checked before the issue goes out. A story whose only link is dead is left out, and the last line says how many links were checked.

An honest comparison

Three other ways to keep your clients up to date, and what each one costs you

By hand

Read the industry press and write it up yourself. Full control. It costs the hours you do not have, and it stops the first busy week.

A newsletter platform

Good at sending and at growing a list. No idea what to say. You still find the news and write every word.

A personal AI summary

Fast, often free, and fine for keeping yourself informed. Written for you alone. Nobody else reads it, and your name is nowhere on it.

LongLens tells your readers what the news in your field means for them, ready to send, as a letter and in every other format. You keep the name, the last word and the readers.

What LongLens does not do

  • Write your own company news. LongLens works from what the press, the forums and the videos in your field say. Your own announcements are still yours to write, and you can add them as a note in any issue.
  • Run ads or paid subscriptions. Use a newsletter platform built for that.
  • Replace you. Your judgment is the point. LongLens puts it in front of more people, issue after issue.
