AI moderators and synthetic users: early data says use them for practice, not evidence

The evidence on AI in research is turning cautious. A small trial shows participants say less to AI moderators, and practitioners report weak results on anything beyond simple studies. Synthetic users look more useful as rehearsal partners than as data.

AI moderators: thin evidence, early warnings

So what: If clients ask whether AI can replace you in interviews, there is now some data. It is small and limited, but it points to shorter, thinner sessions and heavier review work. Position AI moderation as an option for simple studies, with a human reading transcripts, not as a swap for discovery work.
  • Do AI Moderators Uncover as Much as Human Moderators?  MeasuringU
    In a 20-participant US trial, AI sessions ran 37% shorter with 45% fewer words per minute, so depth claims need testing.
  • Limitations of AI in UX Research  applause.com
    Practitioners report extra review time, lost 'aha' moments and weak language support, which are fair questions to put to any vendor.

Synthetic users: rehearsal yes, evidence no

So what: Synthetic participants look too agreeable to stand in for real people, which undermines selling them as research data. They may still help you pilot a discussion guide before fieldwork. Keep real participants for findings, and be clear with clients about the line between the two.
  • UX Roundup: Practice Interviewing With AI | AI Answer Complexity | Date-Picking | NASA-TLX | Why vs. What | Activity Theory | Filtering | Commanding Heights  Jakob Nielsen on UX
    Rehearsal with AI participants led 17 of 21 researchers to revise guides, a cheap pilot step, though it gives no evidence about real users.
  • UX Roundup: AI Persuasion | Fake Users vs. Real Users | Summaries as Dark Design | Agentic Video Production | Feedback | High AI Subscription Churn | Sparring with Voice AI | Silence | Split View  Jakob Nielsen on UX
    Its summary flags that real users reveal what synthetic ones miss, and a US study found user ratings missed AI's persuasive effect, so measure behaviour.

AI and user trust

So what: Stated trust and actual behaviour often diverge, and researchers are well placed to measure the gap. Product teams adopting AI content need evidence on how users react once they know it is AI, not just assumptions.
  • AI-Generated Product Photos Are Putting Off Online Shoppers, Study Finds  petapixel.com
    A Baymard survey of 2,273 US shoppers found 60% less confident buying after learning images were AI-generated, a useful test case for your clients.

Human factors evidence earlier in development

So what: The argument that research belongs at the start, not as a final check, is one you can reuse with product teams. It is a vendor's view, so treat it as a pitch rather than proof.
  • HFE & AI 2026: TÜV Rheinland Advocates Human Factors Research Across Full Product Development Cycle  plataformamedia.com
    A testing lab argues for human factors research from product definition onward, which backs your case for early involvement, though it is promotional.
