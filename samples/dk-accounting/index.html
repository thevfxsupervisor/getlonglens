Corporate tax overpayment window closes soon, and new EU parcel fees add to import costs

The most actionable item is a short window to prepay corporate tax and earn tax-free interest, so check cash position now. Separately, a new EU handling fee on non-EU parcels affects clients who buy stock from abroad. A proposed cap on interest deductions is mainly a personal tax matter.

Tax and interest rules

So what: The corporate tax item is the one with a deadline, so clients with spare cash may want a quick conversation. Details are thin, so check the rules before advising. The interest deduction cap is still being negotiated and mostly hits wealthy households, so it is not yet a client action.
  • Revisionshus: Sidste chance for at tjene på selskabsskat  Borsen
    Clients with spare cash may be able to overpay corporate tax and earn tax-free interest, but the window closes in about two weeks; verify terms first.
  • New tax ceiling to impact just 1.2 percent of Danish taxpayers  The Local Denmark
    Proposed 150,000 kroner cap on deductible interest is still in negotiation and affects about 1.2 percent of taxpayers, so a watch item only.

Hiring skilled staff from abroad

So what: A new work permit route lowers the salary floor for workers from 16 countries, but only for firms with a union agreement and certification. Most small clients will not qualify, since certification needs at least ten full-time employees and two years of operation. It may help larger clients in the book.
  • How will Denmark's new collective agreement-based work permit work?  The Local Denmark
    Only firms with a collective agreement, ten or more full-time staff and certification can use it; the scheme starts in January 2027.

Costs and EU fees

So what: The new EU handling fee raises the cost of ordering from outside the EU, including for business purchases. Clients who import small items should budget for it and expect it to apply to orders placed shortly before the start date.
  • Today in Denmark: A roundup of the latest news on Monday  The Local Denmark
    A two euro fee per item type on non-EU parcels, on top of a three euro fee for low-value parcels, raises costs for clients importing stock.
