Vet prices go public from March 2027, and bird flu means some birds must now be kept indoors

After 22 March 2027 small vet businesses must publish a price list, flea and worming prices and pet care plan details. Written estimates for big bills and a cap on prescription fees follow after 22 September 2027. Bird flu is confirmed in England again, and since 13 November some bird keepers must house their birds.

Vets must publish prices, flea and worming costs and care plan details after 22 March 2027

So what: You will be able to compare. After 22 March 2027, vet businesses with 14 or fewer practices must publish a standard price list for up to 36 common services, the prices of their most commonly sold flea, tick and worming products, and the full details of any pet care plan, including what is included and how to cancel. Expect to see ours on our website. Larger groups have their own CMA guide.
  • Small veterinary businesses: complying with the CMA's Order  Competition and Markets Authority (GOV.UK)
    The Veterinary Services Market Investigation Order 2026 sets four deadlines for small vet businesses: September 2026, December 2026, March 2027 and September 2027.

Written estimates for big bills and a cap on prescription fees follow after 22 September 2027

So what: After 22 September 2027, if your pet's treatment is likely to cost £500 or more including VAT, and a fifth or more of that is for tests or treatment you have not yet chosen, the practice must give you a written estimate first. You must also get an itemised bill. If you would rather buy medicine elsewhere, a written prescription can cost at most £21.40 for the first medicine and £12.70 for each extra one.
  • Small veterinary businesses: complying with the CMA's Order  Competition and Markets Authority (GOV.UK)
    A written estimate is needed when treatment is likely to cost £500 or more including VAT and at least 20% is for tests or treatment not yet chosen; the prescription fee caps of £21.40 and £12.70 apply until 31 March 2028.

Pet owners must be told their end-of-life options, with clear cremation prices, after 22 March 2027

So what: When a pet reaches the end of its life, the practice must tell you all your options: an individual or communal cremation through the practice, arranging a crematorium yourself, or taking your pet home for burial. Prices for individual and communal cremation must be clear, with what is included, and a basic communal cremation must be offered. If you are facing this now, call us and we will talk it through.
  • Small veterinary businesses: complying with the CMA's Order  Competition and Markets Authority (GOV.UK)
    From after 22 March 2027 practices must make owners aware of the full range of options, publish them on their websites, offer a basic communal cremation and give clear pricing for individual and communal cremations.

Bird flu: some hens and other birds must now be kept indoors

So what: Bird flu is confirmed in England again, and since 13 November there is a legal duty to keep some birds indoors. If you keep 50 or more birds, you must house them. With fewer than 50 you must also house them if you sell or give away eggs or birds; if they are only for your own use, you do not have to. If a bird seems unwell, call us at once. If you suspect bird flu, the law says you must report it.
  • Bird flu (avian influenza): latest situation in England  Defra and APHA (GOV.UK)
    An avian influenza prevention zone covers England and Scotland from 11 November, with mandatory housing measures in England from 13 November; there are 10 confirmed cases in the UK this season.
  • Bird flu: rules if you keep racing pigeons, birds of prey and other captive birds  Defra and APHA (GOV.UK)
    The housing rule covers all captive birds including pets, and you must contact your vet immediately if you have any concerns about your birds.

Recall: one batch of Sededorm injection for dogs and cats

So what: A manufacturer has recalled one batch of an injection used in dogs and cats because of mislabelled packaging, a Class III recall. The notice is addressed to wholesalers and vets, who are told to check stock and set the batch aside. If you have a question about a medicine your pet has had, ask us.
  • Sededorm 1 mg/ml Solution for Injection for Dogs and Cats - product defect recall alert  Veterinary Medicines Directorate (GOV.UK)
    Vetpharma Animal Health has started a Class III recall with immediate effect for batch X-006 (Vm 32509/4000, expiry June 2029) because of mislabelling in the packaging.

Animal antibiotic sales are 56% below 2014, but rose 2% last year

So what: Antibiotics only keep working if they are used carefully: when bacteria learn to survive them, infections in pets, farm animals and people get harder to treat. That is why a vet weighs up whether your pet needs one. If you have questions about your pet's medicine, ask us.
  • Stable sales support long-term decline in animal antibiotic use  Defra and Veterinary Medicines Directorate (GOV.UK)
    The UK-VARSS report shows sales of antibiotics for animals up 2% between 2024 and 2025 but 56% below 2014, with resistance in livestock substantially below historic levels.
