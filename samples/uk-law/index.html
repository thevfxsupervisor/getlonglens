Flat owners are due stronger rights over service charges from 1 April 2027, if Parliament approves

If Parliament approves rules laid on 12 November, from 1 April 2027 flat owners in England can demand information about their service charges, including earlier years. HM Land Registry wants views by 22 December on who should see who owns land. From 25 November you can only make a lasting power of attorney online through GOV.UK One Login, and Domestic Abuse Protection Orders reach all of England and Wales. From 1 January 2027 an employee can claim unfair dismissal after six months in the job, not two years.

Flat owners are due stronger rights over service charges from 1 April 2027

So what: If you own or are buying a flat in England: the government said it would put new rules to Parliament on 12 November, and if Parliament approves them, from 1 April 2027 you can demand information about your service charges, including earlier years, and your landlord needs a good reason to refuse. A landlord will also need a court or tribunal to approve passing on its legal costs when you challenge a charge, and you can claw back your own; the detail of that part is still to be laid. If you are buying a flat, ask us to look at the service charge history before you commit.
  • Flat owners handed massive boost to fight rip-off service charges  Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (GOV.UK)
    Regulations to be laid in Parliament on 12 November 2026 come into force, subject to Parliamentary approval, from 1 April 2027 (service charge information, legal costs), 1 November 2027 (insurance information, standard demand forms) and 1 November 2028.

Land Registry asks who should see who owns land, and says views are due by 22 December

So what: The government is asking whether more information about who owns land should be public, what access to Land Registry records should cost, and whether access should be restricted where it could put someone at risk. If you own land or property, these are the records that show you as the owner. You can respond until 22 December 2026, and we can talk it through with you first.
  • Future of access to HM Land Registry information  HM Land Registry, MHCLG, Defra (GOV.UK)
    The consultation covers new ownership datasets, access and charging, public interest access, and powers to restrict access where there is a significant risk of harm; it closes at 11:59pm on 22 December 2026.

Fewer errors in house purchase paperwork, but they still cause delays

So what: After you buy a home, HM Land Registry has to record you as the owner, and mistakes in the paperwork slow that down: a name that does not match your ID, a missing document or an unclear plan. The Land Registry says these avoidable errors fell by almost half between 2022 and 2025. Send us your ID and the exact spelling of every name early, so we can check them before we submit.
  • HM Land Registry publishes new avoidable requisitions data to help cut delays  HM Land Registry (GOV.UK)
    Avoidable requisition points fell 47.7%, from around 839,000 in 2022 to 439,000 in 2025; the newest dataset covers 1 April to 30 September 2026.

Making a lasting power of attorney online needs GOV.UK One Login from 25 November

So what: From 25 November 2026 the only way to sign in to the government's online service for making a lasting power of attorney is GOV.UK One Login. You can keep the LPAs already in your account when you first sign in. If you are partway through making one, finish it and download it for printing before 25 November and you will not need One Login. If you would rather not do it online, talk to us about preparing your LPAs with you.
  • We are changing the way you sign in to Make a lasting power of attorney  Office of the Public Guardian (GOV.UK)
    "From 25 November 2026, you’ll only be able to sign in to your Make a lasting power of attorney account using GOV.UK One Login."

Domestic Abuse Protection Orders reach all of England and Wales from 25 November

So what: Domestic Abuse Protection Orders are court orders made to protect someone from domestic abuse. Until now they were being tried in some areas; from 25 November 2026 they extend to all of England and Wales for specified purposes, and the family court rules on serving them, their conditions and electronic monitoring have been updated to match. If you or someone close to you needs protection, talk to us about whether an order could help.
  • Family Procedure Rules: Practice Direction Update No. 5 of 2026  Ministry of Justice
    PD36ZG is amended to reflect "the extension of the piloted commencement of Domestic Abuse Protection Orders to all of England and Wales for specified purposes from 25 November 2026."

Employees can claim unfair dismissal after six months from 1 January 2027

So what: If you employ people in England, Scotland or Wales, from 1 January 2027 an employee who has worked for you for six months, not two years, can normally claim unfair dismissal, and the cap on compensation goes. If you are an employee, you are protected much sooner. Before you let anyone go, talk to us about the reason and the steps to follow.
  • Employment Rights Act 2025  Acas
    From 1 January 2027 employees can claim unfair dismissal after 6 months in a job instead of 2 years, and the limit on compensation for unfair dismissal is removed.
  • Director information hub: Unfair dismissal and employment tribunals  GOV.UK, Insolvency Service
    Before 1 January 2027 an employee normally needs 2 years of continuous employment; from that date it falls to 6 months, in England, Scotland and Wales but not Northern Ireland.

Employment tribunal claims now have six months, not three

So what: Since 1 October 2026 an employee has six months, not three, to bring a claim to an employment tribunal. If you employ people, a claim can now arrive months later than it used to. If you have a problem at work, do not sit on it: talk to us early. A problem that began before 1 October may still have the three month limit.
  • Employment Rights Act 2025  Acas
    Time limits for an employment tribunal claim rose to 6 months on 1 October 2026; if a problem started before then, the 3-month limit might still apply.

Employers must now take all reasonable steps to stop sexual harassment

So what: Since 30 October 2026 a business must take "all reasonable steps" to prevent sexual harassment of its staff, where the law used to say "reasonable steps", and answers for harassment by customers and clients unless it has done so. If you employ people, check your policy and your training. We can go through them with you.
  • Employment Rights Act 2025  Acas
    Since 30 October 2026 employers must take "all reasonable steps", not just "reasonable steps", and are liable for harassment by third parties such as customers or clients unless they have.
